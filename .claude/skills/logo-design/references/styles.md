# Logo styles

Pick directions that contrast with each other; three from the same family
is one direction, not three.

## Lettermark / monogram
- **Single glyph** — one letter from the brand font, one detail altered (IBM-style restraint).
- **Ligature monogram** — two initials sharing a stroke.
- **Cut letter** — a letter with a notch, slice or missing segment carrying the idea.
- **Stencil** — letter broken into separated segments; strong at large sizes, risky at 16px.
- **Typographic token** — letter plus a symbol (`_`, `.`, `/`, `>`), reads as a code or CLI reference.

## Geometric
- **Swiss minimal** — one primitive (circle, square, bar), strict grid.
- **Bauhaus** — 2–3 primitives in primary colors composed into a figure.
- **Modular / pixel** — built from a 5×5–8×8 grid of cells; crisp at small sizes by construction.
- **Line / stroke** — monoline with constant weight; keep stroke ≥ 8% of the grid.
- **Negative space** — the idea lives in the gap between shapes.

## Symbol
- **Abstract** — a shape that suggests motion, growth or connection without depicting anything.
- **Pictorial** — a simplified real object; needs to be reduced to its silhouette.
- **Emblem / badge** — mark enclosed in a shape; good for stamps, weak as favicon unless the enclosure is the tile.

## Wordmark
- **Neo-grotesk** — the brand's sans set tight, one letter customized.
- **Split weight** — two weights or colors inside one word (e.g. first/last name).
- **Monospace** — terminal/code feel; pairs with developer brands.
- **Script / vintage** — rarely right for tech; include only if the brief says warm or craft.

## Combination
- **Mark + wordmark** — define the lockup spacing in units of the mark's stroke.
- **Tile icon** — mark inside a rounded square; the tile is part of the identity (app icons).
